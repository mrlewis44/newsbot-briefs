4) HW Summit: Unified LLPA grid raises MBS investor concerns

So-what: Removing the 20-pt VS4 haircut is a de facto LLPA cut for many VS4 files, but secondary buyers may still haircut pools. Front-end pricing vs back-end appetite can diverge.

1-minute read

HousingWire's Flávia Furlan Nunes (Oct 1, 4:31 pm) covers investor pushback on FHFA's unified LLPA grid that puts VantageScore 4.0 on the same pricing grid as Classic FICO and removes the prior 20-point VS4 calibration haircut. Pulte's one-grid policy is a de facto LLPA cut for many VS4 files on the front end. Secondary buyers may still haircut pools.

MBA's Pete Killmer: expect higher Vantage utilization, but questions remain on investor appetite for VS4 versus FICO MBS, plus FICO 10T timing. NFM's Greg Sher: not enough secondary data yet. BankSouth's Erin Dee: lenders may price both scores front-end while bond buyers do not, squeezing the middle if secondary disagrees.

KBW color: VS4 was 5.5% of GSE originations in September (flat). Rocket and UWM are about 95% of that volume. Rocket at 34% penetration (VS4 default). UWM at 24.9%. Example math in the piece: 700 FICO / 720 VS was 1.5% LLPA on the FICO path and is now 1.25% on the VS4 path under the unified mapping.

Expand 3-minute read

Policy change in one line: FHFA under Pulte moved to a single LLPA pricing grid so VS4 joins Classic FICO, and the prior 20-point downward calibration for VS4 is gone. That is a front-end pricing win for many files that score better on Vantage. HousingWire's Oct 1 summit coverage centers the open question: will MBS investors treat VS4 collateral the same as FICO collateral, or keep applying their own haircuts?

Industry voices in the piece map the squeeze. Killmer (MBA) expects utilization to rise and flags investor appetite plus FICO 10T timing as unfinished business. Sher (NFM) says secondary data is still thin. Dee (BankSouth) names the practical risk: you can price both scores to the borrower and still get a worse bid from the bond buyer, leaving the lender holding the basis.

Pivot's Jennifer McGuinness frames the mapping as a backdoor LLPA reduction: about 68.7% of the historical VS population would get improved score-based pricing under the unified grid, rising to 72.5% in the 2023 through 3Q25 cohort, with less upfront G-fee compensation and no collateral change. That is why some secondary desks are cautious even when originators like the borrower-facing math.

Volume snapshot from KBW in the article: VS4 share of GSE originations 5.5% in September, flat; Rocket plus UWM about 95% of VS4 GSE volume; Rocket 34% penetration with VS4 as default; UWM 24.9%. The worked example (700 FICO / 720 VS moving from 1.5% to 1.25% LLPA on the VS path) is the whiteboard number for LO training this week.

BuyWise so-what: celebrate better borrower pricing where VS4 helps, but confirm takeout and best-ex before you promise a credit. Front-end LLPA cuts that secondary will not pay become margin problems, not marketing wins. Pair with the TransUnion $0.99 through 2028 card: score choice economics just got a multi-year floor beside the grid change.

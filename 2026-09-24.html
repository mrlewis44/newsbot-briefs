5) Regulators eye a less prescriptive mortgage rulebook

So-what: Directionally friendlier for originators, but changes still need agency picks plus formal rulemaking, not overnight.

1-minute read

HousingWire's Sarah Wolak covers an ACUMA panel with Weiner Brodsky Kider's Fed Kamensky and Bob Niemi. Theme: federal regulators are moving away from technical compliance checklists toward risk and actual consumer harm. Executive orders are directing reviews of origination and servicing rules. That is friendlier direction for brokers, not a free pass.

Rules under review language includes TRID, HMDA, ability-to-repay/QM, loan officer compensation, and mortgage servicing/loss mitigation. The CFPB already issued a July RFI on TRID (tolerances, timing, earlier disclosures). Kamensky's three-word frame: reduce burden, modernize rules, expand credit access.

Timeline honesty matters. Agencies must pick which rules to change and usually run notice-and-comment rulemaking. Kamensky: it will be a while before material impact. CFPB footprint talk: possible ~53% staff cut toward ~515 people and roughly 70 exams per year versus about 600 before. Niemi's reminder: compliance does not change just because examiners are scarcer.

Expand 3-minute read

Lead with what changes for a CA broker this quarter: almost nothing operationally mandatory, plenty directionally useful. Kamensky and Niemi told ACUMA's Make Your Mark conference that supervisors are shifting from technical-violation scorekeeping toward reasonable underwriting and identifiable consumer harm. NCUA comments already emphasize risk-focused exams where management must explain its risk profile. That is a culture shift, not a deleted reg.

Executive-order scope. March EO language on credit access points CFPB at TRID, HMDA, ATR/QM, LO compensation, and servicing/loss-mitigation provisions. CFPB's July TRID RFI asked about tolerance limits, timing, and whether some disclosures can come earlier. Appraisal directives float alternative valuation models, AI tools, and lighter appraisal requirements on lower-risk low-LTV or small-balance loans. ROAD to Housing Act implementation work shifts to state and local zoning/permitting. Appraisal reforms include codifying reconsideration of value and a public appraisal database.

CFPB capacity is the exam so-what. Bureau still lacks a permanent director; Senate Banking advanced Brian Johnson's nomination. Requested RIF could cut staffing about 53% to roughly 515, aligned with new HQ capacity. Most exams expected remote, limited, harm-focused, at about 70 per year versus about 600 under the prior administration. A "humility pledge" reserves bureau attention for patterns of violations that cause identifiable harm. Niemi: do not dismantle your compliance program because exams got rarer.

Servicing and small-servicer notes. CFPB may narrow proposed Reg X loss-mitigation amendments after industry pushback on repeated reviews and language-access burdens. Kamensky floated making permanent flexibility to offer some loss-mit options without a complete application first, and raising the small-servicer exemption from 5,000 loans toward at least 10,000, especially for credit unions that keep servicing for relationships.

Broker takeaway. Track proposed rules and comment periods. Do not rewrite your TRID, HMDA, or LO-comp procedures on speech vibes. Do brief sales teams that the long-term direction is less checklist theater and more harm-based supervision, which still punishes real consumer damage. Source: HousingWire, Sarah Wolak, Sep 23, 2026.

What to tell LOs this week in one paragraph. Executive orders and ACUMA panels are not license to skip TRID timing, HMDA fields, or LO-comp plans. They are a signal that examiners may ask fewer gotcha technical questions and more "show me the consumer harm" questions over the next year. Keep your policies current. Log exceptions. When a rule proposal or RFI opens (TRID already did in July), assign one ops owner to draft comments or at least track the docket.

CA-specific angle. State regulators and investor overlays can stay strict even if the federal exam footprint shrinks. Non-QM and private-money files still need airtight ability-to-repay stories. Servicing clients in wildfire or disaster cohorts should watch CFPB Reg X loss-mit flexibility separately from origination burden relief. Directionally friendlier federal tone plus unchanged state law is the honest combo. Source: HousingWire Wolak / ACUMA panel Sep 23.

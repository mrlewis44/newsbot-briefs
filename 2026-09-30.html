2) HW: GSEs one LLPA grid; 20-pt VS4 adjustment removed

Wed show

So-what: NEW vs Tue Pulte headline: single LLPA matrix for Classic FICO and VS4, and the 20-point VS4 haircut is gone. Classic FICO still an option; FICO 10T not GSE-approved. Broker pricing story for today.

1-minute read

HousingWire deepens Monday's Pulte post. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac will use one loan-level price adjustment grid for mortgages scored with either Classic FICO or VantageScore 4.0. The pricing grid Pulte shared removes the 20-point downward adjustment that had applied to VS4 scores under the early-September dual-score framework.

That is the broker so-what. Same LLPA matrix, no automatic −20 on VS4, and lenders must still use the same scoring model for all borrowers on a loan. Classic FICO remains an option and is still required for manually underwritten loans. FICO 10T is not yet approved for GSE delivery (FHA still plans 10T for January 2027).

Rocket Mortgage said it will use VS4 as its preferred model for eligible direct-to-consumer loans. After testing both models on 1.4 million credit reports, Rocket said some borrowers saw better pricing, with average closing savings about $1,600 where savings printed. UWM has said roughly 25% of borrowers already see a better result on VS4 versus Classic FICO.

Expand 3-minute read

So-what first for wholesale desks. A single GSE LLPA grid that drops the 20-point VS4 adjustment changes who lands in which price bucket, not just which logo sits on the credit report. Tue's brief flagged Pulte's single-grid announcement. Today's HW card is the implementation detail that matters for lock desks.

What changed versus early September. Dual-score approval already let approved lenders choose Classic FICO or VS4 for eligible conventional loans, same model for every borrower on the file. VS4 carried a 20-point downward adjustment to map models. Pulte's shared grid removes that adjustment. Classic FICO stays. Manual UW still needs Classic FICO. FICO 10T is still not a GSE delivery option even as FHA targets January 2027 for 10T.

Lender behavior is already moving. Rocket: preferred VS4 for eligible DTC loans in the fourth quarter after testing 1.4 million reports; average about $1,600 closing savings for borrowers who got a better price path. UWM: about 25% already better on VS4, with talk that share could approach 40% by end of September. VantageScore's public claim of nearly $1 billion in savings over the next 12 months is a company projection, not a GSE guarantee. Say it that way.

Client language. Do not promise every borrower a VS save tomorrow. Do say GSEs are unifying LLPA pricing across Classic FICO and VS4 without the prior −20 map, timing and investor overlays still need confirmation, and we will reprice when your investor updates sheets. Keep adverse-action and score-disclosure hygiene tight on dual-score files.

Wed show

So-what: Lennar CSF −14% over three years, but Miller says cost softening is over in core markets as data centers and immigration enforcement tighten some geos. KB still gets labor; oil/materials are the creep.

1-minute read

ResiClub's labor note is the NEW builder card versus Tuesday's Lennar gross-margin and incentives deep dive. Do not rehash the 15.8% GM / 12% incentives piece. Today's angle is construction cost and labor supply.

Lennar says construction costs per square foot have fallen 14% over the past three years. He pointed at immigration enforcement and enthusiastic data-center construction.

KB Home, reporting days later, is not seeing the same labor squeeze yet. CEO Robert McGibney said starts are down and labor to job sites has not been a big cost issue. The pressure KB is living with is mostly materials and fuel, including direct fuel surcharges that can reverse if fuel pulls back.

Data-center competition is the chart that sticks. In July 2022, about $28 of U.S. data-center construction spent for every $1,000 of new single-family homebuilding. By July 2026 that was about $190 per $1,000.

Expand 3-minute read

Lead with the split read, not a national labor crisis headline. Lennar (No. 2) says labor tightness is showing up in pockets. KB (No. 7) says not on their sites yet. Same cycle, different footprint and negotiating leverage.

Cost math for brokers who sell against new construction. Lennar CSF −14% over three years was the mild relief while land and incentives crushed margins. If that relief ends, builder payment menus can stay aggressive even while vertical costs stop falling. That keeps pressure on resale credits in public-builder metros.

Three watch factors ResiClub flags for the next two years. (1) Data-center buildout competing for electricians, labor, land, and inputs: $28 DC per $1k SF in Jul 2022 versus $190 in Jul 2026. (2) Baby-boomer retirements hitting skilled trades harder after a lost post-GFC training decade. (3) Net international migration boom-to-bust after the 2021–2024 surge (record 2.7 million Jul 2023–Jul 2024; 1.3 million Jul 2024–Jul 2025), with researchers estimating roughly one-third of construction workers foreign-born and a material undocumented share in laborer roles.
